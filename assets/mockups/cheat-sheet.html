One-page field reference

SOC SLA — WHAT THE ANALYST ACTUALLY NEEDS TO KNOW

1When an alert arrives — check in this order

  1. Severity
  2. Asset criticality
  3. Existing active incidents on the same asset/customer
  4. Remaining SLA time
  5. Alert confidence
  6. Customer-specific requirements
  7. Related/correlated alerts
  8. Threat context

2When to escalate

  • It crosses the severity threshold
  • Scope is spreading
  • You're blocked past X minutes
  • Evidence suggests active compromise, not just anomaly

3When to call the manager

  • Multiple P1s at once
  • You are the only qualified analyst
  • A customer dependency is blocking and time is running out
  • You suspect the severity was wrong

4Signs the SLA is at risk

  • Elapsed time crosses 60–70% of the target with no update
  • Case is waiting on a dependency with no ETA
  • Queue depth is rising faster than it's clearing

5How to document a dependency

INC-4471 — paused 02:14, reason: awaiting customer AD admin to confirm account owner, requested via ticket #, resumed 04:02.

6When NOT to pause the timer

  • Don't pause just because you're busy
  • Don't pause without a named, contactable dependency
  • Don't pause retroactively after the fact

7What evidence is required before closing

  • Root cause identified — or explicitly "insufficient evidence, monitoring"
  • Affected scope confirmed
  • Customer notified per SLA tier
  • Disposition (true positive / false positive / benign) justified in writing, not just selected from a dropdown

8What must be in a handover

Case summary Timeline IOCs Affected assets Open questions Next action SLA remaining
9 — What NOT to do
  • Don't close without evidence
  • Don't use "awaiting customer" without actual customer contact
  • Don't downgrade severity to dodge the clock
  • Don't copy-paste generic investigation notes
  • Don't escalate with zero analysis just to hand off the clock
